# DEMON FIGHTER — Modern-Era Seeds
### REVISED 2026-09-11 — Antem-Extinction Overhaul pass

Nineteen post-Consolidation events, seven developed and twelve sketched, spanning the ~275–300 year span since Antarkeia — now the Breach of Antarkeia; see the current Chronicle.

As with everything in this project: breadcrumbs, not canon set in stone. Real-world historical echoes are noted for craft reference only — they are inspirations for shape and theme, not a sequence to be reproduced.

## Seven Seeds, Developed

**The Colehurst Miscast** *(renamed from "The Cindergate Miscast" — House Cindergate is a retired name; House Colehurst is current)* — A descendant of Harroc Colehurst attempts to recreate his ancestor's Antarkeia fire-lance volleys and gets it catastrophically wrong. The site still burns cold and guts fire backward. His notes were burned by his own household.

**The Waking of the Slag Tortoise** — Something enormous and half-fused with molten rock surfaces near the mining town of Cresthollow, sits for nineteen days doing nothing, and vanishes underground again. The town keeps a shrine to it.

**The Sarcophagus That Answered** — Tomb-robbers wake something in a crypt that speaks before it attacks — courteous, precise, unnervingly interested in what year it is. It answers three questions, then kills two of the four robbers.

**The Glass Shard Golem Trade Wars** — Two merchant houses fight over exclusive trade-road rights using constructed golems instead of soldiers. Ends in mutual bankruptcy — the accepted origin point for golem-crafting as a known trade.

**The Emptying of Hollowreach** *(reworked, 2026-09-11)* — An entire settlement goes quiet overnight — doors open, hearths warm, no bodies. What's found inside standing exactly where residents should have been are zombies, same as anywhere else in Faerlan — no separate name, no in-world explanation. *(The original draft called them "Empties" as a named in-world category. That's retired — "Empty" was folded into the existing Bestiary Zombie entry as a description, not a species, and should never surface as a named term to a player.)*

**The Storm Caller's Bargain** — A coastal lord strikes an unspecified bargain to end a decade of ruinous storms. They stop within the week. His descendants still hold the coast and still don't discuss the cost.

**The Sun Priest Schism** — A fire-worship order, its doctrine loosely descended from Colehurst's battle-wizards, splits violently over conquest versus vigilance. At least two modern warrior cults are suspected descendants of the losing faction.

## Twelve More, Echoing Without Mirroring

Real-world touchstone noted in parentheses — reordered and refracted on purpose, not sequenced to match.

**The Concordance of Ash** (echoes: standardized writing) — Merchants, not scholars, forced a common script into being to standardize trade ledgers — now taught as high culture.

**The Raising of the Sundered Spire** (echoes: monumental construction) — A forgettable petty-king conscripts golem-labor to raise a record tower. It cracked down the center the winter it finished and still stands, sundered but unfallen.

**The Reckoning Statutes** *(reworked, 2026-09-11)* (echoes: early written law) — The first formal law addressing Farhand inheritance rights — drafted to unclog noble courts, not out of compassion, and resented for it by Farhand and landed nobility alike. *(Original draft addressed "Kin and Reachborn inheritance." Both species are cut from current canon. Farhand — Faerl-only, claiming unverifiable Antem ancestry — is the surviving concept this kind of dispute actually belongs to: inheritance claims complicated by a Farhand petitioner's contested lineage story is a direct, natural fit for the original beat, and keeps the "resented, not compassionate" framing intact.)*

**The Siege of Emberfall** (echoes: the fall of Troy) — A coastal city besieged nearly a year over a stolen betrothal-relic nobody remembers anymore. Every surviving version of the song about it ends differently. *(Note: this is a different Emberfall event from "The Refusal at Emberfall," now in the Chronicle of Antarkeia — that one is the site of a pre-march negotiation, ancient and unrelated to this siege. Worth flagging if the two are ever placed on the same map.)*

**The Colehurst Dominion** *(renamed from "The Cindergate Dominion")* (echoes: a great empire's rise and fall) — A later Colehurst descendant briefly rules more territory than any house since Consolidation, then collapses within two generations from simple overreach.

**The Open Council of Widow's Rest** (echoes: birth of democracy) — A Widow's Accords sect quietly begins electing a rotating local council instead of enduring hereditary lords. Unofficial, illegal, four generations strong.

**~~Kaelen Voss and the Bright March~~ — retired, 2026-09-11.** This entry described "a young Kin commander" sweeping through six rival holds within a decade, dying young of illness, his empire fracturing. Two problems: Kin is cut from current canon, and — more fundamentally — the Calendar/Faiths/Houses/History Corrections document already states that Iskrel Sixhold "fully replaces the discarded 'Kaelen Voss' placeholder — new name, **new backstory**." Iskrel Sixhold's actual story is Antem, set in the ancient Scattering Wars, thousands of years before Antarkeia — not a modern-era Faerl or Kin event at all. This isn't a name that needs updating in place; the character this slot was built around has already been moved to a completely different era and species elsewhere. The modern-era "conqueror who dies young, empire fractures" slot is now open if a future session wants to fill it with something that actually belongs in the post-Consolidation timeframe.

**The Free Port of Saltmere** (echoes: founding of the Roman Republic) — A trade city bloodlessly deposes an inept hereditary lord for a guild-elected magistrate council, and never lets anyone forget it hasn't needed a lord since.

**The Hundred Years' Contest** (echoes: the Punic Wars) — Two houses fight three generations-apart wars over one mountain pass. By the third war, neither side can articulate why it still matters.

**The Prophet of the Long Fast** (echoes: a major religious founder) — A wandering preacher, forty days unfed in the Desert zone, returns teaching demon-hunting as covenant rather than survival. Fragments of his language survive in modern hunting orders that don't know the source. *(Cross-reference: this is very likely the same figure as "The Long Fast tradition" / "the desert Prophet" already established in the Calendar document's Faiths table — that entry dates him to deep Antem-adjacent prehistory, not the modern era. If so, this entry is misplaced the same way Kaelen Voss was, just less severely — not fixed this pass, flagged for the next person who touches this file.)*

**The Reaving Years** (echoes: the Viking Age) — Vantry Isles sailors abandon trade for open coastal raiding for two generations, then stop as abruptly as they began, for reasons no record agrees on.

**The Parting of the Widow's Faith** (echoes: the Great Schism) — The last unified Widow's Accords remnant splits irreparably over whether men can be true equals or only guests. Both factions still claim to be the true keepers.
