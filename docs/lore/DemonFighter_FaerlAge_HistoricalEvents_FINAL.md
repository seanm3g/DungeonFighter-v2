# DEMON FIGHTER — The Faerl Age: 52 Historical Events & Session Decision Log

*Reference document. Companion to `DemonFighter_HouseGenealogies_FINAL.md`, split out separately for ease of updating. Builds on the locked Faerl Age era structure (Landfall Centuries → Warlord Centuries → Widow's War → Charter Centuries → Gathering Crown → Long Fracture → House Centuries → Consolidation → Year Zero) and the existing Antem-Extinction Overhaul canon. Each entry carries a consequence, an institution, or a lie that outlived it, per standing doctrine. Where an entry touches an existing figure or event, it's marked (LOCKED) and left unmodified.*

## PART ONE — 45 EVENTS

*Creature tie-ins noted in brackets where used — drawn from the ~77 bestiary entries not yet tied to an event.*

### Landfall Centuries (5,150–4,000 BY) — 5 entries

1. **The First Waterskin.** A Landfall-era camp survives a three-season drought only because one family refused to share their reserve — reviled at the time, credited generations later as the unspoken root of Dray's entire water-discipline culture, long before there was a House Dray to claim it.
2. **The Scarab Wardens.** Early Faerl settlers near the desert's edge notice that graves left undisturbed by scavengers all share one thing: an infestation of oversized, territorial beetles nesting nearby. They begin deliberately encouraging the nests near burial ground. *[Guardian Scarab]*
3. **The Quiet Threshold.** A Farhand family is the first on record to refuse entry to their dead relative's return — a superstition against welcoming anything back through a door it had already left through. The custom outlives the family by millennia, now just "don't answer a third knock."
4. **The Salt Road's First Toll.** Two unrelated camps meet at a natural salt-flat crossing and, rather than fight over it, invent the concept of a toll — paid in meat, tools, or labor. Neither camp remembers inventing anything; they just remember it was always done that way.
5. **The Long Bark.** Faerl herders first notice a wild canine that howls only at dusk, in a pattern too structured to be random — three rising notes, always the same three. They start listening for it as a storm-warning. It's right often enough to matter. *[Dogkin]*

### Warlord Centuries (4,000–3,200 BY) — 8 entries

6. **The Ninefinger Reckoning.** A warlord missing four fingers from a training accident builds a reputation on being harder to kill than a whole man — eventually giving his diminished name to what becomes a Dray vassal hold (LOCKED: Ninefinger).
7. **The Marsh Accord.** Two rival warlords, unable to take a contested wetland by force, agree to alternate control season by season. It works for six generations before anyone remembers why, then collapses the first year someone forgets to hand it back on schedule.
8. **The Weeping Wolves.** A Faerl hunting band, cornered by an unusually large wolf pack, survives only because the pack breaks off the attack at the sound of a horn — a domesticated-stock instinct nobody alive understands, credited instead to a local storm-spirit. *[Wolf]*
9. **The Warlord Who Counted Wrong.** A petty chieftain, convinced by a miscounted census that his warband outnumbered a rival's two to one, attacks and is annihilated. "Counted wrong as [his name]" becomes shorthand for confident, fatal error — the name itself lost to time, the idiom outliving it.
10. **The First Roof-Oath.** A warband leader, tired of losing men to petty desertion, institutes the practice of publicly swearing loyalty under a literal roof rather than open sky — the deep, unremembered root of what becomes the Shared Roof institution three thousand years later.
11. **The Ash-Grey Herd.** A Faerl scout reports cattle near a demon-scarred field turning the same washed-grey color as Hallow's Bend's missing villagers (LOCKED) — except the cattle are still alive, still grazing, apparently unbothered. Nobody ever explains it. The herd is left alone. *[Pale Cattle]*
12. **The Warlord's Debt to No One.** A rare warlord who refuses all forge-debt entanglements, paying cash or labor for everything, dies wealthy and unallied — his hold absorbed within a decade by neighbors who'd extended him no favors to call in. The lesson later chroniclers draw: independence and isolation cost the same price.
13. **The Naming of the Groundcallers.** Faerl settlers first document a burrowing creature that seems to respond to rhythmic stomping — used deliberately, generations later, to flush game or warn off worse things. *[Groundcallers]*

### The Widow's War (3,200–3,050 BY) — 3 entries, satellite to the existing trio

14. **The Unweighed Grain.** During the worst of the war's famine years, a single granary town refuses to hoard against its neighbors, distributing stores freely even as its own people go hungry. It survives, barely, and becomes the model the Widow's Accords later cites when arguing for shared reserves.
15. **The Last Warlord's Bargain.** A minor warlord, facing certain defeat, offers his own sons as hostages in exchange for his people's safety — an early precedent the Widows draw on when structuring their own council's authority over surviving men.
16. **Beghar's First Lie.** Years before his revolt, Beghar Flintbrow is recorded telling a border patrol he'd "seen nothing" in the desert he'd just returned from — already lying, already changed, and nobody thought to ask twice. The line "seen nothing, like Beghar" becomes an idiom for a lie so smooth it isn't even suspected.

### Charter Centuries (3,050–2,800 BY) — 4 entries

17. **The Widow's Blush Toll-Keepers.** The first formal wardens of the preserved forest trade route (LOCKED) are appointed — a role that becomes hereditary, then ceremonial, then, within two centuries, entirely forgotten as anything but a place-name.
18. **The Drowning Choir.** Sailors along the coast begin reporting a sound like birds crying just beneath the waterline before a storm — real, native, and eventually used as an informal weather-warning by anyone willing to wade out and listen. *[Drowning Birds]*
19. **The Second Charter, Unfunded.** In the wake of House Dravencourt's collapse (LOCKED), a rival coastal warlord proposes a second Vantry expedition — and can't raise a single backer. No one sails again for generations. The refusal itself becomes a minor legend: "wiser than Dravencourt, or just poorer."
20. **The Mirror Deer Cull.** A Faerl hunting tradition develops around a deer whose antlers grow in near-perfect bilateral symmetry — prized, hunted almost to local extinction within a generation, then protected by informal taboo once someone notices how rare the symmetry actually was. *[Mirror Deer]*

### Gathering Crown (2,800–1,800 BY) — 7 entries

21. **Verah Kestrel's First Refusal.** Before founding the Crown, Verah is offered marriage into an already-powerful clan that would have given her authority without a fight. She refuses it, insisting on earning the claim instead — the founding myth's most-repeated detail, whether or not it happened exactly that way.
22. **The Common Ledger.** Kestrel's Crown formalizes the Concordance of Ash script (LOCKED) into an empire-wide tax and trade record — the first time in Faerl history anyone can prove, on paper, what they're owed.
23. **The Nearhorse Levy.** The Crown standardizes mounted messenger service using a smaller, hardier breed better suited to long relay routes than warhorses — the origin of every later "royal road" network the Houses inherit and maintain without knowing who built it. *[Nearhorses]*
24. **The Nine Governors' Oath.** The Crown divides its territory among nine appointed governors, sworn directly to the throne rather than to each other — a structure explicitly designed to prevent exactly the kind of regional consolidation that later becomes the four Houses. It works for six generations.
25. **The Quiet Purge.** A Kestrel-line heir, suspicious of a governor's growing popularity, has him quietly removed rather than publicly tried — the Crown's only well-documented act of court violence, and the first crack in its reputation for even-handed rule.
26. **The Laughing Pack's Warning.** Crown border-scouts document a pack predator whose calls mimic human laughter closely enough to lure the unwary — first dismissed as folk exaggeration, then confirmed, then simply avoided. *[Laughing Pack]*
27. **The Writ Before the Writ.** A full century before Tamrin Kestrel is forced to sign away central power, an earlier heir voluntarily delegates tax authority to regional governors during a succession crisis — a precedent Tamrin's opponents cite directly as proof the Crown had already conceded the principle once.

### Long Fracture (1,800–1,000 BY) — 7 entries

28. **The Governors Who Kept the Roads.** In the decades after the Writ of Tamrin, most of the nine governorships collapse into local warlordism — except three, which keep maintaining the old royal road network out of simple habit, not loyalty. Those three roads are the ones still usable, in fragments, in the present day.
29. **The Vantric Cadet Line.** A minor desert clan, distant kin to no one yet, begins the practice of marrying strategically across neighboring territories rather than warring for them — the same clan Ilvet Dray's daughter later marries into (see Genealogies).
30. **The Second Crossing's Quiet Return.** *(LOCKED, redated — see collision audit)* — a Vantry-contact expedition returns confirming distant kinship rather than conquest or trade.
31. **The Drel Migration.** A native grazing species, previously confined to a single valley, spreads across three territories within two generations after a hard winter forces new grazing patterns — Faerl settlers follow the herds into land they'd otherwise never have scouted. *[Drel]*
32. **The Forgotten Governor's Line.** One of the original Nine Governors' bloodlines survives, unnoticed and unranked, into the Long Fracture — a minor noble family with a stronger paper claim to the old Crown than any of the eventual four Houses, never pressed, never even fully aware of what they're sitting on.
33. **The Toxic Drinker's Season.** A desert mutation — a creature that drinks poison to fuel its own venom — spikes in population one particularly bad drought year, killing enough livestock that three minor holds are abandoned outright. *[Toxic Drinker]*
34. **The Withering's Quiet Cousin.** *(satellite to the main Withering plague, ~1,050 BY)* — a smaller, localized wasting sickness strikes one river-valley community forty years before the main Withering event, caused by a similarly cracked relic. Chronicle-Keepers later treat it as an overlooked early warning nobody connected until it was too late.

### House Centuries (1,000–500 BY) — 8 entries

35. **The Ford Sentinel's Cousin.** A second, smaller relic-guardian is discovered and destroyed near a Marlow river-crossing roughly a century before the more famous Ford Sentinel (LOCKED) — treated as an unrelated curiosity at the time, only connected by scholars centuries later.
36. **The Colcross Founding.** House Colcross (LOCKED, Thorne vassal) is formally established, generations before Halric Colcross — founded by a minor lord who took in refugees from two collapsed rival holds under one roof, giving the House its motto and sigil (LOCKED) long before the Shared Roof made "one roof, two hearths" an institution.
37. **The Edge Stalker's Toll.** A blade-wielding hunter-creature begins preying specifically on solitary travelers along mountain rims bordering Dray territory — enough disappearances that Dray formally bans solo travel on three named passes, a law still nominally on the books. *[Edge Stalker]*
38. **The Rock Lobber's Wall.** A Dray vassal hold builds its outer wall unusually high after repeated stone-hurling attacks from a creature nesting in nearby cliffs — the wall outlives the threat by centuries and is now just considered good desert architecture. *[Rock Lobber]*
39. **The Marlow-Thorne Salt Dispute.** A generations-long dispute over a salt-marsh boundary is finally resolved not by force but by both Houses independently losing interest once a better salt source is found elsewhere — remembered less as diplomacy than as mutual boredom.
40. **The Scavenger Rat Plague.** A population boom of wasteland vermin overruns two Dray border holds in a single bad season, spreading a minor but persistent illness that becomes endemic — "rat-fever" remains a recognized, non-fatal seasonal ailment in desert territories to this day. *[Scavenger Rat]*
41. **The Altitude Wraith's Pass.** A mountain pass connecting Thorne and Dray territory is abandoned after separate trading parties report the same cold, aimed, disorienting presence — the pass is never reopened, and its old name is deliberately not recorded here. *[Altitude Wraith]*
42. **The Sun Priest Founding Proper.** The Sun Priest order (LOCKED) formally splits from Colehurst's battle-wizard tradition — its doctrinal schism (conquest vs. vigilance) begins here, generations before the "at least two modern warrior cults" the existing entry references.

### Consolidation (500–0 BY) — 5 entries

43. **The Summit Titan's Silence.** A stone-and-ice giant, long rumored to guard a mountain summit near Thorne's border, is climbed to and found — nothing. No remains, no explanation, just an empty summit and a story that loses its ending. Climbers still make the attempt periodically, expecting nothing and getting it. *[Summit Titan]*
44. **The Deserter Shade's Warning.** A soldier's ghost, said to haunt a stretch of desert he fled through generations earlier, is reported with increasing frequency in the decades right before Waelbrook falls — dismissed as unrelated at the time, cited afterward as an omen nobody heeded. *[Deserter Shade]*
45. **The Last Governor's Line, Pressed.** The forgotten Nine-Governors bloodline (see #32) finally presses its old paper claim to the Crown, decades before the Breach — too late, too quiet, and too easily absorbed into House Marlow's own territory to matter to anyone but the family itself. A final, quiet epitaph for the Gathering Crown, extinguished not by war but by irrelevance.

## PART TWO — SEVEN MORE: THE CROSSOVER ADDITIONS

*The fantasy/sci-fi crossover scan approved eight items; the Mistborn-style "founding legend has a messier true story" idea was always a future game-content hook, not a drafted event, and stays that way. The remaining seven are drafted here. The Dray founding oath is folded directly into the genealogy document rather than listed here, since it's a lineage fact, not a standalone event.*

46. **The Betrayal at Greymoot** *(Landfall Centuries, ~4,700 BY)* — An early Faerl stronghold, larger and better-defended than anything else built in that era, falls in a single night when one of its own gatekeepers opens a side passage for a rival camp he'd secretly been trading with for years. The stronghold's survivors scatter rather than rebuild, and the era's already-thin trust in permanent settlement hardens into active avoidance — the deep, mostly-forgotten root of why Farhand culture in particular never builds anything meant to last.

47. **The Waking at Drossglen** *(Warlord Centuries, ~3,600 BY)* — A warlord, having recovered a relic core intact enough to still respond to touch, deliberately tries to wield it as a weapon against a rival camp rather than simply carry it as loot. Whatever it does instead kills his own warband and leaves a crater at Drossglen still faintly warm today. No formal law follows — none is needed. "Don't wake what's sleeping" becomes not superstition but absolute, near-universal practice — the real reason nobody in this world experiments with relics.

48. **The Solvane Truce** *(House Centuries, ~785 BY)* — In the immediate aftermath of Sella Dray's repulsion of a Colehurst raiding party (see Genealogies), a Colehurst kinsman, Rothgar Colehurst — not the paramount line itself — proposes a truce-feast at the Dray hold of Solvane to formally end hostilities. The feast turns to slaughter mid-toast; most of the Dray delegation present dies before realizing the offer was never sincere. The betrayal outlives Rothgar's own House standing — he's disowned within the year — but "a Solvane welcome" survives in both Houses' speech ever after, meaning any hospitality too generous to be trusted.

49. **The Standing Ban** *(House Centuries, ~700 BY)* — Generations after Drossglen, the four Houses formally commit to never turning recovered relics against each other in war — not out of trust, but out of mutual fear that any single House willing to break the taboo first would force every rival to follow. Quietly upheld ever since; no House has ever openly tested it, and no chronicler has ever recorded why not.

50. **The Split at Wierston** *(House Centuries, ~600 BY)* — Two Marlow cadet branches, generations removed from any shared living memory of why they're rivals, go to open war over succession at Wierston itself — the House's founding seat. Neither branch wins outright; the eventual "reconciliation" is really exhaustion wearing a treaty's clothes, and the paramount line that emerges afterward is, by more than one chronicler's account, a compromise candidate nobody particularly wanted.

51. **The Vantry Reckoning** *(House Centuries, ~900 BY, generations after the Second Crossing)* — A Vantry-descended pocket, grown unusually prosperous on an island sitting over unusually intact old-world ruins, reaches for something in those ruins its own elders had always left alone. What follows destroys most of the island in a single event no surviving account agrees on the shape of. Later Vantry contact with Faerlan stays guarded and infrequent ever after — not fear of Faerlan, but a hard-won caution about digging too deep into what the ruins actually are.

52. **The Marten Line's Quiet Watch** *(ongoing, background texture only)* — Non genealogical tradition holds that Marten's own direct descendants — the same Marten who witnessed the Crash firsthand — have quietly kept some presence, however thin, near every major flashpoint in Faerl history since, consistent with the Non's already-locked pattern of showing up at the edges of major events. Nothing dramatic is drafted here; this is a standing background thread future sessions can activate whenever a Non presence would enrich a scene, not a discrete event with its own date.

## LOCKED THIS SESSION — decisions to carry forward

- **Precursors and Antem are the same entity** — "Precursors" is the Genesis-text-era term for what current canon calls Antem; already noted in `Reference_CONSOLIDATED.md`.
- **Non motive:** restoring Ark 1's original mission intent, with the knowledge base necessarily imperfect/degraded over 36,000 years rather than pristine — this is what makes their apparent omniscience compelling rather than a plot hole. Self-interest plausibly steers them toward guiding Faerl society upward, which also explains their total disinterest in anything beyond the planet.
- **Faerl in the present have zero memory of arriving by ship** — that knowledge warped into a waking-on-the-planet origin myth/religion. The Non, with Earth-tech access and longer institutional memory, retain a truer but still-eroding version of events.
- **"Scattering Wars" stays Antem-only.** The Faerl civil-war material previously sharing that name (Widow's Accords, Beghar Flintbrow, the Charter of Salt and Silence) needs its old era-label references updated to the new Faerl Age era names — not done yet, flagged below.
- **Year Zero ≈ Genesis Yr 63,500**, locked — the bridge number reconciling the Reference document's absolute "Genesis Year" timeline with the "years before Year Zero" convention used everywhere else.
- **The Faerl Age era structure** (Landfall Centuries → Warlord Centuries → Widow's War → Charter Centuries → Gathering Crown → Long Fracture → House Centuries → Consolidation → Year Zero) replaces the old flat "Age of Man" label.
- **House magic-visibility correction:** magic becomes *more* noticeable and harder to deny as Antarkeia's containment fails, not less — this was backwards in an earlier draft of the era table.
- **Aldros → Tamrin Kestrel; Corin Ashvane → Varic Sennor** — name collisions caught and fixed before anything was filed.
- **Faerl pronunciation:** rhymes with "feral," not "fairl."
