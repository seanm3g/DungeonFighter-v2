**DOCUMENT PRECEDENCE**

This document supersedes species descriptions in `DemonFighter_Reference_CONSOLIDATED.md` (formerly split across four now-retired files) where they conflict. The Genesis text and all locked historical events (Chronicle of Antarkeia, Hollowing, Rivening, Calendar) remain governed by their own canonical documents — this pass touches them only where they assert present-day Antem/Vessel population status, flagged separately. **SPECIES OVERVIEW**

Three cognizant populations exist on Faerlan in the modern era (approx. YT 275–300): Faerl, Non, and Farhand (a social identity, not a species). Two further populations — Antem and Vessel — are extinct, known only through ruins, relics, and legend. "Empty" is retired as a species/lore category entirely, its texture folded into the existing Zombie bestiary entry (see below). Kin are cut from canon entirely (see below). None of the surviving populations are equally distributed, equally visible, or equally understood by the world's dominant culture. The Faerl are the player character lineage and the dominant political force.

Quick reference:

*Faerl — Dominant human population. Ark 2 descendants. Medieval feudal society.*

*Antem — EXTINCT (~4,000–6,000 years before Year Zero). The original people of Faerlan. Known only through ruins, relics, pictographs, and legend — no living population, no present-day contact.*

*Farhand — Faerl social identity, not a species. Rootless, occupation-defined underclass, concentrated in border territories. No longer explained by literal Antem interbreeding (see below).*

*Vessel — EXTINCT. The androids/Antem-bonded beings, gone with the Antem they served. Known only through ruins, remains, and — deliberately — murals and pictographs depicting a strange servant class that appears to have been human.*

*Empty — RETIRED as a species/lore category. Folded into the existing Zombie bestiary entry; no in-world name or exposition survives.*

*Non — Ark 1 descendants. Same species as Faerl, a distinct breed. Extremely rare, secretive, present in some capacity at nearly every major historical event. Operate in deliberate shadow.*

*Kin — CUT. See note at end of document.*

**Faerl**

**ORIGIN**

Descendants of Ark 2 — humans who refused emotional extraction and were sent to Faerlan as a consequence or by choice. They arrived with almost nothing and built a feudal medieval society from scratch. They are the youngest population on the planet in relative terms and the least adapted to it, which is part of what makes them the underdog player lineage.

**POPULATION & ROLE**

The dominant species — in the modern era, the *only* politically organized species. They run the houses, the armies, the trade routes, and the political institutions. The world as the player experiences it is a Faerl world, full stop; every other population is either extinct, mythologized, or exists at the margins in ones and twos.

**APPEARANCE**

Fully human. No distinguishing physical traits. Skin, hair, and eye tones span the full human range. The only visual markers are cultural — house sigils, clothing quality, the wear patterns of their profession.

**SOCIAL POSITION**

The Faerl regard the Antem the way we regard any long-vanished civilization — a mix of superstition, romanticism, and unease about ruins best left alone. They treat Farhand as an underclass, regard chance encounters with Empties as you'd regard any dungeon horror (not a "people," a hazard), and are wary and suspicious of Non when they're recognized at all — which is rare, by design. The Proven Right — the Faerl settler-colonial doctrine — underpins most of their territorial behavior, now aimed at empty land and ruins rather than a living rival population.

**Antem — EXTINCT**

**ORIGIN**

The native humanoids of Faerlan — the original people, the builders of Antarkeia. The name was given to them by the Vessels and adopted; they either forgot their original name or never rendered it in any language that survived. Their true name for themselves is genuinely untranslatable into Faerlish and is never rendered on the page. No living voice remains to correct any record about them — everything known is secondhand, filtered through ruins, relics, pictographs, and generations of Faerl legend, in the spirit of what the modern world actually knows about Bronze Age civilizations: plenty of monuments, almost nothing about the texture of daily life.

**EXTINCTION**

Gone roughly 4,000–6,000 years before Year Zero (The Fall) — not destroyed in a single catastrophe, but declined the way a civilization declines: plague, the loss of their Stillwing mounts, internal civil war over what remained, and a final fragmentation into clan groups too small and scattered to sustain anything resembling continuity. They did not go out fighting anything. They went out managing something — the demons they had spent generations containing — until managing it cost them everything. By the time Faerls converged on Antarkeia at Year Zero, there was no one left to meet them. The city was already a ruin; the "invasion" was a breach, not a battle. (See the Hollowing/Rivening and Chronicle of Antarkeia rewrites for the full arc — this entry only needs to reflect the outcome.)

**POPULATION & ROLE IN THE MODERN ERA**

None. No living Antem exist, no enclaves, no diaspora. What remains: ruins (Antarkeia foremost, but scattered satellite sites across the continent), relics and artifacts (the substrate for a large share of rare item flavor), pictographs and glyphs (environmental storytelling, degrading-resolution history), and Faerl legend — folk memory descended from the sparse, ancient period when early Faerl arrivals and Antem briefly and occasionally crossed paths, filtered and reshaped over millennia the way any oral legend is. A modern Faerl "knowing about" the Antem means knowing a story shaped like the Odyssey, not a fact.

**APPEARANCE, CLOTHING, AND VISUAL PROMPT**

Unchanged from the prior canon *as a description of what surviving art depicts* — this is now understood in-world as reconstruction, not observation. Humanoid, standing at human height. Arms noticeably longer than human proportions — fingertips reach mid-thigh when standing relaxed. Hands wide and long-fingered. The face is the most distinctive element: a smooth continuous brow ridge of bone beneath the skin, sweeping laterally outward and slightly upward from the bridge of the nose, flush with the skull, terminating above the temple — not a protrusion, not a horn, just pronounced bone structure pressing outward beneath skin, architectural in quality. Eyes large and slightly elongated at the outer corners, irises in deep amber-gold with a faint luminous quality in low light. Nose broad and low-bridged. Mouth wide. Ears larger than human ears, with a subtle outward curve to the outer helix and a soft irregular point at the tip — functional and organic rather than graceful, proportional to the face. Skin tones run warm and deep. The overall impression is striking rather than monstrous — beautiful and not-quite-human simultaneously.

Garments as depicted in surviving fragments and murals: layered robes in forest greens and deep ochres, structural weave detailing, braided cords at the wrists incorporating dried leaves, river stones, small carved fragments. Barefoot or sandaled.

**THE ANTEM AND THE PLANET**

Their relationship to Faerlan was spiritual and biological in ways the Faerl don't have language for. They were here longest. The planet shaped them and they shaped it. This bond was not magic in any way that would break the world's concealed logic — it was simply the result of being the original people of a world for long enough that the world was in them. Framed in past tense now, throughout — this is a people spoken *of*, never *with*.

*Full visual prompt retained from prior canon, reframed as a reconstruction/mural reference rather than a living-subject portrait: Full body character concept art, realistic painted style. A humanoid figure at human height with noticeably long arms — fingertips reach mid-thigh when standing relaxed — and wide, long-fingered hands. The brow bone is heavy and dramatic — a smooth continuous ridge of bone beneath the skin, sweeping laterally outward and slightly upward, flush with the skull, no protrusions, no points. Beneath it the eyes are large and slightly elongated at the outer corners, irises deep amber-gold with a faint luminous quality. Nose broad and low-bridged. The ears are larger than human ears — proportional to the face, not oversized — with a subtle outward curve to the outer helix reminiscent of a fruit bat's ear, the tip coming to a soft irregular point, functional and organic rather than graceful. Clothing layered robes in forest green and deep ochre. Braided cords at both wrists with dried leaves and river stones. Sandals. Background is ancient forest, low dappled light. Full body visible. No horns. No pointed fantasy elf ears.*

**Farhand**

**WHAT THEY ARE — REVISED**

A Faerl social identity, not a species and not a lineage. Previously defined as the product of Faerl/Antem interbreeding — that origin is no longer possible under the revised timeline and is retired. Farhand are now purely Faerl: a rootless, occupation-defined underclass concentrated in border territories and trade routes, the Boneyard-adjacent wanderers, guides, and go-betweens nobody asks too many questions about. Their identity of "belonging nowhere" is now built on class and geography rather than blood.

*What survives from the old framing, deliberately:* a persistent, unverifiable Farhand self-mythology that their bloodline carries some old, diluted trace of the vanished Antem — an explanation for their rootlessness, their comfort with ruin-adjacent work, their naming customs' attunement to natural phenomena. Nothing in this world confirms or denies it. It functions exactly like a modern family legend about a great-great-grandmother who was "part something exotic" — almost certainly untrue, told anyway because it explains something the family wants explained. This preserves the flavor of the old biological premise as folklore without requiring it to be biologically real.

**NAMING & CULTURE**

Unchanged. Farhand carry a given Faerl first name and a two-word spirit name in place of a surname — verb+noun or adjective+noun, regionally flavored. Forest/Thorne territory: cold, root, frost, breaking wood. Desert/Dray: heat, dust, mirage. Fire/Colehurst: forge, cinder, smolder. Water/Marlow: current, silt, reed, flood. The spirit name is given at birth or coming-of-age by community tradition, not inherited. Example: Serel Farhand becomes Serel Breaks the Current.

**POPULATION & ROLE**

Distributed throughout the world, concentrated in border territories and trade routes. The Boneyard-adjacent, rootless, occupation-defined underclass. Skilled, capable, trusted by nobody institutionally and by specific people completely.

**VISUAL PROMPT — FINAL**

*Unchanged, since the appearance was never actually exotic — this reads as an ordinary Faerl now, no adjustment needed: Full body character concept art, realistic painted style. A human figure, weathered from outdoor work and travel. Clothes assembled rather than chosen — heavy oiled-wool cloak over layered rough-spun in earth tones, belt hung with practical tools, boots resoled at least twice. No house sigil. No heraldry. Worn dagger at the hip, handle wrapped in cord. Posture loose and self-sufficient. Background is open road, forest edge. Full body visible.*

**Vessel — EXTINCT**

**ORIGIN & BIOLOGY**

The androids sent to Faerlan before human arrival. Not mechanical — grown. Engineered organic tissue, designed to be approachable, birthed from something resembling a womb with personalities and facial structures deliberately crafted for kindness. Their biology was real biology, but wrong in subtle ways: organ arrangement slightly off, bone material denser and differently colored than human bone, no evolutionary leftovers — no appendix, no wisdom teeth. Everything inside was exactly what was needed and nothing else.

**THE BOND**

Vessels were bonded to Antem by instinct that predated reason. The in-world legend — across all Faerl cultures now, since there's no other source left — is that the angels who walked the world did not all leave; some stayed, chose the Antem, and remained. The Faerl word *beholden* — reserved specifically for this relationship — is the closest any language gets to naming it. Past tense throughout now: the bond, and everything it explained, ended when the Antem did.

**POPULATION & ROLE IN THE MODERN ERA**

Extinct — no living Vessels remain. Their decline tracked the Antem's own: the Hollowing and the Rivening devastated the communities that sustained Vessel society, and by the time of Antem's final fragmentation, the bond had nothing left to be bonded to. What's left are remains and ruins: inert or destroyed units found in Antarkeia's ruins and satellite sites, and the tech/biology substrate that explains a meaningful share of the world's rare loot and "techno-echo" creature design.

**Confirmed direction:** Vessels should show up prominently in pictographs, murals, and ruin-art — depicted as a strange servant class that appears to have been human, standing alongside the Antem in scenes a modern Faerl can't fully interpret. This is deliberate mystery-fuel, not an oversight — nobody in the modern era has the context to know what they're looking at.

**VISUAL PROMPT — FINAL**

*Unchanged, understood now as depicting a ruin-era figure, not a living encounter: Full body character concept art, realistic painted style. A figure that reads as human and produces a faint wrongness that takes several seconds to locate. Face designed to be approachable — regular features, pleasant expression. Skin real but too even in tone. Clothing very old, repaired across multiple eras of available material. At the collar and one wrist where outer layer has worn through, the tissue beneath is visible — looks like skin and muscle but the color is slightly off, too uniform. Not metal. Not mechanical. Just wrong. Background is a forest clearing, late afternoon light. Full body visible.*

**"Empty" — RETIRED AS A SPECIES ENTRY, FOLDED INTO THE ZOMBIE BESTIARY ENTRY**

Resolved: this is no longer a named creature-people in its own right. It survives only as flavor-text texture on the existing **Zombie** entry in `DemonFighter_Bestiary_v2_ThreeTier.md` (General Reckoning tier, tagged Genesis-adjacent) — players fight zombies, never "Empties." No in-world name, no lore label, no exposition.

The fold is clean: the existing Zombie entry already describes decay that's "slower and wronger than flesh should," hollowed from within, echoing a half-forgotten plague — that's the right instinct, it just needs the Empty's specific texture merged in so the wrongness reads as *engineered-gone-wrong* rather than *ordinary undead*. Recommend the Bestiary entry absorb: organic (not mechanical) deterioration, tissue that discolors in patches of grey-brown and pale yellow beneath worn skin, and — the strongest single detail — a face that still holds the ghost of an expression that was designed to be approachable, which is what makes it disturbing rather than merely gruesome. No player-facing text should ever explain *why* a zombie has this quality. It should just consistently, quietly not read as a normal corpse come back wrong — it should read as a *product* come back wrong.

This also means: no "legend" text about angels or bonds breaking survives as its own doctrine — that explanation only made sense when Empties were understood as failed Vessels specifically. Cutting it is a feature, not a loss; the strangeness reads better unexplained.

**Non — REVISED**

**ORIGIN**

Descendants of Ark 1 — Earth's best and brightest, the farmers, engineers, and mathematicians sent ahead. **Reframed:** the Non are not a separate species from the Faerl — they are the same species, a distinct *breed*, the way a greyhound and a pug are both dogs. Tens of thousands of years of separate lineage on this planet, apart from the Ark 2/Faerl line, account for the differences below without requiring biological divergence.

**WHAT THEY ARE**

A secretive knowledge-caste, closer to a cleric class than a hidden species. Deeply, disproportionately versed in the planet's real history — the Antem, the Crash, the Hollowing, all of it — and they seem to have some presence, direct or once-removed, at nearly every major historical event Faerlan has recorded. Their motives for this are never made explicit. Neither is how they know what they know.

**WHAT THEY KNOW AND WHY THEY DON'T SHARE IT**

The Non have run the calculation and concluded that sharing doesn't work. Not once, not reliably. They are old enough — individually and as a lineage — to have watched people receive correct information and collapse anyway. The Antem civil war. The Hollowing. The Forgehand Rising. In every case the information existed. Someone knew. Knowing didn't save anything.

What they believe instead: the planet has a trajectory — not a destiny ordained by anything that cares, but a direction it was moving before the Demon Crash interrupted it. Their function, as they understand it, is to very gently and very patiently apply pressure in the right places to get it back on track. They are playing a game measured in centuries with pieces they can barely influence.

What makes them quietly sinister: their judgment about what the planet's correct path looks like is not subject to appeal or verification. They might be completely wrong about what they are steering toward, and nobody would know until it was too late to matter.

**APPEARANCE**

Physically indistinguishable from Faerl in the ways that would show up in a crowd — same range of build, skin, hair. The tells are subtler: completely hairless (no eyebrows, no lashes, no stubble), skin unusually unmarked, and eyes that don't quite focus on the room they're in — pale, clear, tracking a longer timeline than the present moment contains.

**POPULATION & ROLE**

Extremely rare. They do not aggregate. They operate individually, embedded in other communities, usually passing as something else. Not an institution, not a faction. A handful of very old, very patient people with an agenda nobody else can fully read.

**VISUAL PROMPT — FINAL**

*Unchanged: Full body character concept art, realistic painted style. A figure that appears human and gives nothing else away easily. Average height, average build, unremarkable in every physical dimension. Face clean-featured and regular. Completely hairless: no eyebrows, no lashes, no stubble, the scalp smooth. Skin pale with a faint warmth underneath, unmarked. Eyes pale, extremely clear, focused on something slightly beyond wherever the viewer is standing. Dressed in plain dark clothing, well made, perfectly fitted, nothing worn or mended. Hands folded. No weapons. No tools. Background is a stone interior, single candle light source. Full body visible.*

**Kin — CUT**

Removed from canon in full. Previously defined as Antem/Vessel hybrids, unable to interbreed outside their own kind, existing in small enclaves. Cut because the concept no longer pulls narrative weight under the revised timeline and raised more unanswered questions than it resolved. No remnant, no legend, no ruin-substrate role retained — unlike Antem/Vessel, Kin simply never existed in this version of canon.

**LOCKED RULES — SPECIES-WIDE**

No species name takes 'The' as a prefix in-world. Veln, not The Veln. Antem, not The Antem.

The word 'Ark' never appears in-world under any circumstances.

The Antem's true name for themselves is genuinely untranslatable into Faerlish. It is never rendered on the page. No living Antem exists to render it even if the rule were broken.

Vessels were beholden to Antem. The word *beholden* is reserved for this relationship and does not apply to other institutions or bonds. Past tense — the relationship ended when both species did.

Non never explain themselves. If a Non character seems to know something they shouldn't, the text does not explain how. The reader files it away.

The retired "Empty" texture (now the Zombie bestiary entry) contains no metal, no mechanical parts. Deterioration is organic only. Nothing about it should break the world's concealed logic.
