# 1. PROJECT OVERVIEW

Demon Fighter is an original sword-and-sorcery video game and narrative universe set on a world called The First Child. The game is a text-based RPG with ASCII/dungeon-crawler aesthetics featuring room-by-room exploration, turn-based combat, and a stat system (STR, AGI, TECH, INT). The project is being developed collaboratively with a friend group including Max (Waxmink).

The core creative vision has a hidden science fiction skeleton (the Genesis text) that underpins all in-world phenomena — magic, demons, imbued weaponry. This sci-fi layer should surface to players only through subtle concept clues. Dedicated fans could eventually reconstruct the lore through inference rather than exposition.

Design philosophy note: technology IS the magic of this world. Characters in-world cannot perceive this. The strangeness of the world is a direct function of how much time has passed since the Genesis events — the longer the gap, the stranger and less explicable things have become.

# 2. THE GENESIS TEXT (CREATOR KNOWLEDGE ONLY)

This is the hidden foundation of the world. No character in the game knows this history. It is ancient beyond reckoning. The word 'Ark' must never appear in-world.

## The Origin

Humans from Earth discovered a planet already inhabited by a humanoid species (referred to as Precursors — their real name lost). Rather than conquer outright, Earth sent robots first, then androids, to prepare the planet for human arrival. The Precursors advanced rapidly with robot help but also adapted the technology for their own purposes, including warfare.

## The Arks

Ark 1 carried Earth's best and brightest — farmers, engineers, mathematicians — held in cryo-suspension for transit. Those who survived and their descendants are the Non: rare, elder figures of exceptional capability, essentially an unbroken thread back to a specific, vanished version of pre-extraction Earth.

Ark 2 carried humans who refused emotional extraction — the most tenacious survivors of labor camp wars, launched essentially as exiles rather than colonists, with minimal equipment. They are the Faerl: the player character lineage, living in a medieval feudal society they built from nothing after landing.

## The Demon Crash

Earth's global government decided their failure was caused by human emotion. They extracted all emotion — fear, anger, grief, wonder, all of it — and launched it at The First Child as a payload, smaller and faster than Ark 2. It arrived first. The world tore itself apart. What the inhabitants now call demons are those emotions, made manifest. The payload also carried wreckage — android tech, ark fragments, corrupted equipment. Demons formed around that debris. What they carry is fossilized human history that nobody on The First Child has the context to recognize.

## The World Today

By the time the game starts, all of this is forgotten history — so ancient it may as well have never happened. The world has regressed to near-antiquity. **The Antem (formerly called Precursors in this document — see the current Species Canon for the locked name) are extinct**, gone roughly 4,000–6,000 years before the game's present, known only through relics, ruins, and legend. The species present in the game's actual world today are Faerl (Ark 2 descendants, the player-character lineage), Non (Ark 1 descendants, a rare knowledge-caste), and Farhand (a Faerl-only underclass claiming unverifiable Antem ancestry). *(This section previously listed "Half-Breeds (Kin)" and "Androids (Vessels/Empties)" as present-day populations — both retired. Kin is cut entirely from current canon, not even as legend. Vessels are extinct, alongside the Antem they were bonded to. "Empty" is no longer a named species at all — it was folded into the existing Bestiary Zombie entry, with no in-world name or exposition. See the full Antem-Extinction Overhaul decision log for the complete reasoning.)*

# 5. DEMONS

Demons are extracted human emotions made manifest after the crash event. They are not a supernatural threat — they are an ecological one. They corrupt land, fauna, and people. Fighters aren't heroes on a quest. They're exterminators, scavengers, and survivors doing what the world demands.

The loot demons carry is the only access ordinary people have to materials and technology beyond what their primitive society can produce. Nobody knows what it really is. They just know it works. Items should not make complete sense to the people of this world — like someone from the middle ages handling something they can't name but know is powerful.

# 6. GAME MECHANICS

## Core Stats

STR (Strength), AGI (Agility), TECH (Technique), INT (Intelligence). Combat thresholds: Hit, Combo, Crit, Crit Miss. Base stats: Damage, Speed, Armor, Health.

## Classes & Weapons

- Barbarian — Mace

- Warrior — Sword

- Rogue — Dagger

- Wizard — Wand

Dual-class combinations unlock hybrid titles: Warbrute, Ravager, Ragecaller, Duelist, Spellblade, Hexblade. Class tier prefixes: Lesser, Blooded, Dread, Abyssal. Quad-stat tiers: Prophet, Godkiller, Worldbreaker, Eternal.

## Enemy Archetypes

Brute, Assassin, Sage, Trickster, Acrobat, Duelist, Knight, Berserker, Warlord, Artificer.

## Item Naming Structure

[Material Prefix] [Adjective Prefix] [Item] [Common Suffix] [Uncommon Suffix] [Uncommon Suffix]. Example: Bronze Nimble Log of Protection of the Tortoise caught in the rain. The further right you go the more it sounds like a story. The further left the more it sounds like a thing. The poetic suffixes (caught in the rain, stitched by a mother, given by dying hands) are where the world's lore bleeds in without ever explaining itself.

## Status Effects

Stun, Poison, Burn, Bleed, Expose, Weaken, Slow, Vulnerability, Harden, Pierce, Confuse.

# 7. NARRATIVE PRINCIPLES

- The sci-fi skeleton stays hidden. The Genesis text informs the world's logic but should never be exposition-dumped.

- No 'Ark' references in-world. Ever.

- Lore surfaces through implication — room descriptions, enemy names, item flavor text, NPC encounters.

- Narrators sound like regular people, not genre archetypes.

- Do not over-explain mechanics in narrative.

- Maintain original paragraph structure in rewrites.

- Do not recycle the same lore devices across different characters or sections.

- Items in this world should feel incomprehensible to those who carry them — like a medieval person holding high technology.

- Characters should feel like you don't want to lose them. Death is always possible.

# 10. MAX'S NARRATIVE QUESTIONS (ANSWERED)

## Where did the demons come from?

When humanity decided their emotions were the reason their mission to The First Child failed, they extracted them and launched them at the planet like a payload. They crash-landed. The world tore itself apart. What the inhabitants now call demons are those emotions, made manifest.

## Why do they have items?

The payload wasn't just emotion. It carried wreckage — android tech, ark fragments, corrupted equipment from the original mission. Demons formed around that debris. What they carry is essentially fossilized human history that nobody on The First Child has the context to recognize anymore.

## Why do fighters have to kill them?

The world regressed to near-antiquity after the crash. Demons aren't a supernatural threat — they're an ecological one. They corrupt land, fauna, and people. Fighters aren't heroes on a quest. They're exterminators, scavengers, and survivors doing what the world demands of them to live in it.

## Loot as the means to the end

The loot demons carry is the only access ordinary people have to materials and technology beyond what their primitive society can produce. You kill a demon, you inherit a piece of something ancient and incomprehensible. Nobody knows what it really is. They just know it works.

# 11. THE TIMELINE (CREATOR KNOWLEDGE ONLY)

Full in-universe chronology from first contact to the game's present-day collapse state. Built to be physically defensible under sub-light-only travel (faster-than-light travel is explicitly ruled out). Distance to The First Child is fixed at approximately 500 light-years — this single value underpins every transit calculation below and should not be changed without re-deriving the full chain. All years are measured from Year 0 = Genesis detection (Earth's satellite first identifies the planet as habitable).

## Key Design Rules for the Timeline

- Distance is fixed at ~500 light-years. All speeds below are fractions of light speed under strictly sub-light travel — no FTL, ever, for tonal and scientific consistency.

- Unmanned craft (robots, payload) can travel faster than crewed craft — no life-support or deceleration-for-passengers constraints. The payload (~11% c) is the fastest thing ever launched in this history — small, brutal, purpose-built.

- Crewed craft (Ark 1, Ark 2) travel conservatively (~2% c) and use cryo-suspension for transit — passengers age normally only after landing, not during the journey. This keeps arriving crews psychologically 'frozen' at their launch mindset rather than culturally drifted like a generation ship would produce.

- Earth's decisions are consistently made on pride/confidence rather than confirmed data — Ark 1 launches before any return signal, and the Schism itself repeats this same institutional flaw at civilizational scale. This is a deliberate thematic throughline, not a plot convenience.

- Named vignette witnesses (e.g. Marten) are never load-bearing for timeline dates — fixed points in this table (e.g. the Year ~36,050 crash) should never be adjusted to fit a witness's personal biography. Witnesses are illustrative, not structural.

- This timeline is bedrock only. Players never see it, never need to see it, and the ~20,000-year post-collapse gap before Ark 2's arrival exists specifically to make total forgetting of this history fully plausible.

# 12. TECHNOLOGY AS MAGIC — POST-COLLAPSE FLAVOR PRINCIPLES

Technology is the magic of this world. No character can perceive this. The degree of strangeness attached to any given phenomenon, creature, or item should scale with how much time has passed and how many hands/generations/environmental forces have reshaped it since the Genesis events.

## Item Tiers as a Function of Preservation, Not Power

Robot- and android-era technology is roughly 45,000+ years old by the time Ark 2 lands. Nothing mechanical survives that span intact as a functioning machine — but the most durable components (dense alloys, sealed power cores, processing cores) outlast everything built around them by orders of magnitude. This gives item rarity an internal logic: common items are Precursor-forged, using scavenged material in functionally ignorant ways (a blade with a metal core because metal is hard, not because anyone knows what it originally was). Higher-tier items are ones where the original core component survives closer to intact — meaning rarer loot isn't 'more magical,' it's less filtered through tens of thousands of years of reinterpretation. The closer to the source, the stranger and more powerful it feels, without ever needing an in-world explanation.

## Example Seeds (not canon, illustrative only)

- A rare staff topped with an 'orb' — actually a cognition/processing core removed from an android's skull cavity tens of thousands of years ago and mounted on a stick by someone who had no idea what it was, only that it did something.

- A blade powered by a fission source pulled from the heart of a long-dead terraforming machine — 'nuclear' only to us; to the world, just a blade that's warm and shouldn't be.

- A creature lineage descended from robots that were 'domesticated' like livestock — over 45,000+ years, integrated into the biological world to the point that what looks like fur is moss growth over what looks like bone but is structurally metal. Reads as animal to the player; isn't, quite.

## Earth/Ark 2 Loadout

Ark 2 is not a colonization ship — it is closer to a deportation vessel for the losing side of a civilizational war. The extraction faction has no incentive to arm or equip people they consider both a liability and an embarrassment. Ark 2 should be treated as bare-bones: whatever the Faerl's ancestors built, scavenged, or smuggled after landing is what exists — not anything Earth issued them. This is the origin point for Faerl feudal society: not a civilization handed tools, but one that reinvented everything with its hands after being discarded.

# 13. THE ENCOUNTER MADLIB TEMPLATE (DEV-APPROVED)

This is the core structural system for room-to-combat encounter generation. It was presented to and approved by the dev team as validated structure — not a draft. It is the scaffolding the actual mechanics of the game should be built around, and should be treated as load-bearing in the same way the Timeline (Section 11) is.

## Concept

An encounter is built from three swappable 'skins' layered over fixed mechanical bones: an environmental phenomenon (the room's sensory hook), an enemy archetype skin (how that phenomenon visibly manifests on the mechanical enemy underneath), and status-effect reaction language (how the environment visibly responds to combat actions, keyed to the actual status effects in the data). The mechanism behind any given phenomenon (why the crystals pulse, why the sap is blue) is never explained to the player — it only needs to be internally consistent to the creators. This mirrors the Genesis text discipline at encounter scale rather than world scale.

## The Template

*You push through [ZONE_DESCRIPTOR] — the kind of quiet that makes you walk softer without deciding to. [ENVIRONMENTAL_PHENOMENON] threads through everything here: [PHENOMENON_DETAIL_1], and if you stand still long enough, [PHENOMENON_DETAIL_2]. Nobody built this. Or if they did, nothing here remembers it.*

*You can press on, or turn back.*

*[If press on:] The [PHENOMENON] gets [INTENSITY_SHIFT] the deeper you go, until it isn**'**t background anymore — it**'**s the room. [SPECIFIC_SENSORY_DETAIL: a smell, a sound, a temperature]. Then you see [ENEMY_ARCHETYPE_SKIN], and something about it is wrong in a way you can**'**t quite name. [SMALL_TELL] — not enough to explain anything, just enough that you notice.*

*[If fight:] You open with [ACTION_NAME]. [ENVIRONMENTAL_REACTION_TO_ACTION — phenomenon visibly responds]. The [ENEMY] [BEHAVIORAL_RESPONSE — unbothered / staggers / doesn**'**t seem to notice].*

*You try [STATUS_EFFECT] next. [PHENOMENON] [COLOR/MOTION_SHIFT_KEYED_TO_STATUS]. [ENEMY_REACTION — matches whether the status actually lands].*

*You draw [WEAPON/ITEM] — [ITEM_ORIGIN_TELL, oblique, never fully explained]. [FINAL_EXCHANGE_DESCRIPTION]. When it**'**s done, [AFTERMATH — what**'**s left in the room, what isn**'**t].*

*[Loot:] [ITEM] comes free of the [ENEMY_ORIGIN_POINT]. It**'**s [PHYSICAL_DESCRIPTION], and it does [MECHANICAL_EFFECT], and neither you nor anyone you could ask would be able to tell you why.*

## Worked Example

You push through a stand of white-barked trees that shouldn't be white — the kind of quiet that makes you walk softer without deciding to. A faint blue sap threads through everything here: it beads along the bark and never quite drips, and if you stand still long enough, you'll swear the nearest trunk leans toward you, then thinks better of it. Nobody built this. Or if they did, nothing here remembers it.

You push on.

The sap gets heavier the deeper you go, until it isn't background anymore — it's the room. The air starts to taste faintly metallic. Then you see it in the branches above — long-limbed, too many joints in the wrong places, wearing something that might be fur and might be moss. Something about it is wrong in a way you can't quite name. It doesn't blink the way a spider should. It doesn't blink at all — not enough to explain anything, just enough that you notice.

You open with a stab. The bark weeps harder with the hit. It doesn't stagger.

You try poison next. The sap along the nearest branch turns a deeper, angrier blue, spreading outward like it's the thing that's been poisoned, not the creature. It slows. Barely.

You draw an arrow you pulled off a corpse three towns back, fletched with something that isn't feather — you couldn't say where it came from before that, only where you found it. It goes in clean. When it's done, the branches are still weeping, and the thing in them isn't anything anymore.

A bone-white shard comes free of the hollow where its chest was. It's warm, and it hums faintly if you hold it too long, and stops if you don't, and neither you nor anyone you could ask would be able to tell you why.

## Data Gaps This Template Exposes (see Workstreams)

- No field currently exists in ENVIRONMENTS for phenomenon-to-status-effect reaction language — this is a cross-reference between a room's phenomenon and a status effect's application that has to either become a new lookup table or live entirely in the prose layer.

- No flavor-text column exists on WEAPONS, Prefix, or Suffix sheets — ITEM_ORIGIN_TELL and MECHANICAL_EFFECT currently have nowhere to live in structured data.

- Room descriptions in the existing ENVIRONMENTS sheet are functional placeholders, not filled to this standard yet — the field exists, the content in it does not yet do what this template calls for.
